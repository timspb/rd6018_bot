# R20-A03 — Controlled comparisons: pulse charging, ordinary cycling, and reversible capacity loss
Date: 2026-11-28. Status PARTIAL. No hardware experiments or charger changes.
## A03-S01 source — Cooper 2002, West Virginia University
Robert B. Cooper, MS thesis, DOI 10.33915/etd.1223, https://researchrepository.wvu.edu/etd/1223/ . Official abstract verified; official PDF returned HTTP403. Chapter 4 and 5 text examined in publicly indexed third-party transcription https://de.scribd.com/document/696122839/Pulse-charging-lead-acid-batteries-to-improve-performance-and-rev . Not visually verified original tables; source status FULL_MACHINE_TEXT_MIRROR, original not archived.
Twelve Optima Red Top 800S AGM batteries in six condition-matched pairs (matching voltage, impedance and discharge time; random selection of candidates does not establish random assignment of treatment). Baseline and eight subsequent comparison cycles, 24-hour rest, resistive 0.1 ohm discharge to 10.765 V for 0.4 seconds. Endpoint is high-rate discharge time, NOT C20 or standardized CCA. Control is ordinary full-wave rectifier charger, itself ripple-bearing; not an ideal DC control. No post-treatment months/EFC or independent diagnosis of sulfation.

Four of six controls improved; pulse improved in three pairs. In pair C the conventional battery alone increased discharge time by 41.51%, versus 52.07% pulse. Relative contrast +10.56 percentage points, not 52% causal gain. Pair D's published 140.345% ratio of percentage improvements is not a 140% capacity recovery; actual changes ~2.60% vs 6.26%. Pair B has unequal starting points and near-ceiling pulse result. Pair F's floor endpoint and assumed sulfation are not verified electrochemical diagnosis. Counts: pulse wins 2, control wins 3, tie 1. No population efficacy claim, equivalence or significance inference from heterogeneous one-pair strata. The thesis is an independent counterexample to universal pulse superiority; not proof that every pulse waveform is ineffective.
## A03-S02 source — Lam et al., CSIRO, 1995
L.T. Lam et al., J Power Sources 53 (1995) 215–228, PII 0378-7753(94)01988-8. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0378775394019888 ; original article PDF mirror https://vershv.narod.ru/sdarticle.pdf (full text, visually checked pp.218–219 Figs 2–5). Controlled experimental 2 V three-plate cells, Pb-Sb or Pb-Ca-Sn grids, reserve-capacity high-rate discharge, 50% rated-capacity endpoint, not automotive C20 or used intact 12 V battery. Charge-to-discharge ratio 1.2; temperatures and grid/cure explicitly differ. 
Fig.2, G2 Pb-Ca-Sn 3BS: ordinary charge 22 cycles; one pulse regime only 8 cycles; other two pulse regimes 77 and 79 cycles. G4 matched geometry/cure: ordinary 8 cycles, pulse 57 cycles. Thus a favorable waveform is not evidence that arbitrary pulsing works; unfavorable parameter choice can shorten endurance. Comparison changes average charging current by an order of magnitude as well as waveform. Attribution to waveform alone not established. Same mean current across pulse variants is more informative for parameter sensitivity, still limited replication.
Fig.5, G3 Pb-Sb: at approximately 80% initial reserve capacity (after 6 cycles), a switch to pulse restored near-initial performance within another five cycles. Switching back to conventional charging caused renewed decay. At ~60% the second pulse intervention only arrested deterioration, did not restore initial capacity. Final return to invariant current reached cutoff. This is induced premature capacity loss with within-cell switching and carryover, not proof of irreversible aged AGM/Ca-Ca restoration or persistent gains after treatment withdrawal. Proposed mechanisms involve PAM crystallinity/accessible surface and resistive PbO-rich interface layer; not generic sulfate-crystal destruction. Article abstract claims three-to-fourfold cycle-life increase under selected conditions; do not collapse grid types or ignore negative regime.
## A03-S03 source — Fraunhofer ISE 2002
Georg Bopp, Rudi Kaiser, Dirk Uwe Sauer, annual report 2002, p.55, “New Charging Procedure for Batteries in Stand-Alone Power Supplies”. Official full PDF https://www.ise.fraunhofer.de/content/dam/ise/en/documents/annual_reports/ISE_JB02_e_COMPLETE.pdf ; original printed p.55 and Fig.1 visually verified. One OPzV 200Ah battery stored unused for five years returned to nominal capacity after three additional constant-current charging phases; subsequent ordinary-charge cycles shown around nominal. No exact followup calendar or independent matched restoration control stated. Separate field comparison 16 sealed-lead-acid PV systems, IV vs IVIa; authors report 5–20% extra usable charge depending on type/season, and little capacity loss in selected high-quality batteries after four years with periodic full charging. This is field maintenance/prevention, NOT four-year follow-up of the single recovered OPzV. Annual report is primary institutional summary with limited raw data, co-operation with manufacturer; do not count as independent replication or universal voltage/current specification. Authors found no general significant pulse-charge benefit for flooded cells in their tested range, with unfavorable lifetime under some non-circulated conditions. Do not transfer stationary OPzV protocol to automotive AGM.

## Evidence integration
C102 Cooper: matched pairs establish both ordinary-cycle improvement and heterogeneous pulse-versus-control results for high-rate discharge time, not C20; limited E2/E3 because mirror only, short horizon, comparator waveform and baseline differences.
C103 Cooper: relative improvement ratios can greatly exaggerate small contrasts; compare actual before/after endpoints and percentage-point contrasts; no claim of statistical equivalence.
C104 Lam: within specified 2 V experiments pulse selection changes cycle endurance strongly, including a negative regime. Mean current differences confound comparison to conventional control; E2/T3 for automotive application.
C105 Lam: early PCL recovery can vanish after return to ordinary charging; later intervention may only arrest decline; not universal persistent restoration. E2/T3.
C106 Fraunhofer: long-stored stationary OPzV recovery and separate four-year maintenance observations support investigation of full recharge/conditioning, not four-year recovery proof; E3/T3.
H65 Apparent recovery is stage-dependent: early reversible PCL can improve while late structural/interface degradation may only stabilize. Independent matched repeated endpoint study needed.
H66 At equal net Ah, charge time, average current and thermal conditions, waveform-specific benefit depends on grid alloy, cure, defect and waveform; compare independent replicate cells.
H67 A standardized ordinary full-charge/conditioning run before specialized treatment may account for a meaningful portion of reported recovery in aged intact batteries; confirm with C20, CCA, post-withdrawal storage/EFC controls.
