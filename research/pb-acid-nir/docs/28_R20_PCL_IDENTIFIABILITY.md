Shiomi et al. 2003 JPS 113:271–276 DOI 10.1016/S0378-7753(02)00522-0, https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0378775302005220: PbSO4 in corrosion layer, poor adhesion, local acid concentration and oxide phase influence interfacial resistance. Full papers inaccessible in this pass (publisher robots); abstract/preview only. No extraction of unverified numerical results. Mechanistic distinction: sulfate accumulation in bulk NAM and sulfate formation in a resistive grid/PAM interface can coexist, but imply different location and causal pathway. No claim that all PCL is sulfate or that charge can repair mechanically lost contact.
Counterpoint: Rand et al. 1996/97 PII 0378775396023063 https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0378775396023063 argues barrier presence alone insufficient; porous material close to collector can lose conductivity. PCL-1/PCL-2 are historical hypotheses, not two mutually exclusive measurable diagnoses. Ruetschi 2004 DOI 10.1016/j.jpowsour.2003.09.052 describes coupled aging (corrosion impedes current, thereby promoting sulfate). Abstract only for this pass. No universal percentages.

## Mathematical boundary: terminal electrical identifiability
For a lumped passive linear small-signal model, Z(s)=Rwire+Rgrid+Rinterface+Rsolution+Rct/(1+s*tau). If two cases have equal total series resistance, Rct and tau, all frequencies and all linear current transients are identical. Swapping contributions of grid and interface leaves the same Z, so even ideal full EIS cannot uniquely recover spatially indistinguishable resistances without extra constraints. This is an exact counterexample within the assumed circuit, not proof that all real EIS cannot distinguish any defects. Example synthetic series components in mOhm: case A grid=1, interface=4; B grid=4, interface=1; common wire+solution=3; common Rct=5, tau=0.02s. Both Z identical. Independent reference electrode, imaging/chemical study or independently validated features may disambiguate; no terminal-only universal chemical classifier. DC step measurements are less informative than EIS and must be labeled by time window. C20/CCA/charge acceptance and safety are separate endpoints.
Decision use: electrical features may support screening, decline detection or estimated capacity with calibrated uncertainty; if multiple mechanisms consistent, return UNKNOWN/MIXED rather than forced label. No destructive examination or hardware action authorized.
